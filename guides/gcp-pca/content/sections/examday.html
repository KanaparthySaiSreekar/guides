Logistics, pacing and technique

Exam day

Since 2 March 2026, Google Cloud exams are delivered by Pearson VUE. You still register and manage the exam through Google’s certification portal (CM Connect), and you don’t need a separate Pearson account.

The week before

  • Re-read all four case studies, then recall each requirement-to-service table from memory.
  • Take one last full mock under exam timing.
  • Check that your government ID matches your registration name exactly.
  • Online (OnVUE): run Pearson’s system test on the same computer and network you will use. Use a wired connection if you can, and close VPNs and other apps.
  • Test centre: confirm the location and arrival time.

On the day

  • Check in early. Online check-in includes photos of your ID and your workspace.
  • Online: a clear desk, one monitor, nobody else in the room, and no notes, phone or smartwatch within reach.
  • Don’t count on a scheduled break. Check the current candidate rules before you start.
  • Case studies open in a split-screen pane during their questions.

After you submit

  • You see a provisional pass or fail on screen. The official result follows by email, typically within 7–10 days.
  • The certificate is valid for 2 years.
  • Retake waits: 14 days after the first attempt, 60 days after the second, and one year after the third, with at most four attempts in two years. Each attempt is charged in full.
  • Renewal: the renewal exam (adds 2 years, opens 60 days before expiry), or Google Skills courses or badges in the final year (adds 1 year).

Pacing for 120 minutes

CheckpointWhere you should be
0:00Start. Don’t read the case studies in full; you already know them.
0:40About question 20. Flag anything over 3 minutes and move on.
1:25All questions answered once, flagged ones included (best guess).
1:25–1:50Revisit flagged questions.
1:50–2:00Make sure nothing is blank. There’s no penalty for guessing.

Reading a question

  1. Read the last sentence first to see what is being asked: design, first step, or most cost-effective.
  2. Mark the constraints: cost, operational overhead, time, compliance, “Google-recommended,” existing skills or tools.
  3. Eliminate options that break a stated constraint or add work the question didn’t ask for.
  4. Between the last two, choose the one that meets every constraint with the most managed, least-privilege, documented approach.
  5. For case questions, check the option against the case’s business and technical requirements and its executive statement.
  6. On “choose two,” each choice must be correct on its own.

Tie-breakers Google’s answers tend to follow

  • Managed or serverless over self-managed.
  • A built-in product feature over custom code or scripts.
  • Preventive controls (organization policy, VPC Service Controls) over detective ones (scripts, reviews).
  • Least privilege, groups and no keys over broad roles and shared credentials.
  • Automation and IaC over console clicks.
  • Incremental, lower-risk change (canary, waves, strangler pattern) over a big bang.
  • Measure first (Migration Center, SLOs, Recommender) before you buy or redesign.
  • Mitigate first (roll back), then find the root cause.
  • The current product names: Agent Platform, Cloud Run functions, Sensitive Data Protection.
